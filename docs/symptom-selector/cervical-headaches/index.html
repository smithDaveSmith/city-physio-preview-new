Upper cervical spine

Headaches from the neck

The upper neck can refer pain into the head. Headaches from this source are known as cervical headaches.

They usually follow poor posture, an injury to the neck, or sustained tension in the neck muscles.

Specific testing of the upper cervical joints can confirm whether the neck is the source of your headaches, which matters because it changes the treatment entirely.

What it probably isn’t. Most headaches do not come from the neck. A headache that starts at the base of the skull, is one-sided, and is provoked by neck position or sustained posture is the pattern that points to the neck.

When not to wait for physiotherapy

Headaches that are new, severe, or different from your usual pattern should be assessed by your GP first. Some symptoms need urgent medical assessment rather than physiotherapy — including loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness around the groin or inner thighs, weakness in both legs, severe pain following a significant injury, or pain alongside fever or unexplained weight loss. Contact your GP or an emergency department first.

If you are not sure, call us on (01) 628 0855 and we will tell you honestly whether we are the right people to see.

General safety guidance, consistent with HSE and NHS public advice. Not a diagnosis.

What happens if you book

Every appointment starts with assessment, not treatment. Your physiotherapist takes a history, examines you, and then tells you what is wrong, what treatment is needed and roughly how many sessions it should take.
